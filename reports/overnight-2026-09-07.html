dnd-kit: main took the other fork, and the agent argued against its own work

The port-rail agent then verified its own {delay:500, tolerance:5} is looser still — a press moving 4.5px and dwelling activates it while long_press never fires — and concluded plainly that its design fails PEP 0013's constraint. It declined to claim credit for the exclusivity fix, on the grounds that its lab never had the three owners coexisting, so it has no evidence either way.

Needs you: The recommendation is sequencing, and it needs your call: do the <b>exclusivity fix</b> as its own change, and let that introduce a gesture-owner registry keyed on select-tool state — with dnd-kit as one implementation and a native StateNode as another. Folding the two dnd-kit contexts together then comes nearly free. As a pure refactor it is not worth doing; as the vehicle for a defect fix it is. It changes shipped Behavior Tree behaviour, so it wants your sign-off, not just a green build.

APCA licensing — the only non-engineering item

The theme scanner implements APCA from scratch. Two problems: it was removed from the WCAG 3 draft in July 2023 (the code called it 'drafted for WCAG 3'), and its licence purports to bind implementations of the algorithm, not just Myndex's source — non-commercial only, commercial use requiring a signed agreement. Writing our own doesn't obviously escape it, and routing through colorjs.io doesn't either, since its APCA path points back to Myndex.

Default I took: Kept the maths, fixed the words. The comment now says APCA is non-standardized, used only as an internal ranking signal, never a compliance claim. Nothing is blocked. If you want it gone, WCAG 2.1 math has zero encumbrance and already sits beside it.
Needs you: BAM is a company. This wants a real legal read, not my opinion.

Six domains, ~7,500 lines of hand-written logic, picked by measuring the largest modules rather than guessing. The headline: dnd-kit was a specific miss, not a pattern — auto-layout has been on ELK.js and d3-hierarchy since 2026-09-02, and five of six domains came back "keep ours" with reasoning that holds up.
