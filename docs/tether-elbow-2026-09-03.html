The interior tether, as an elbow

Render only, Port view only — and the tangle turns out to be the curve's fault, not the tether's. 2026-09-03.

Why the crossings disappear, and when they would come back. The rows run top-to-bottom in the same order the ports run left-to-right. So each tether's horizontal segment stops at its own port's column — short of every later tether's column — and each vertical rises in a column no other tether's horizontal reaches. The routes are monotone in both axes, so they cannot cross. That is a property of the default order, not of elbows: drag one port past another (the V4 stress board) and the two tethers that now disagree with their rows will cross exactly once — which is arguably the right behaviour, since the crossing is the board telling you the reading order no longer matches the signature.

The two constraints you added

Render only. It takes no pointer events, so it cannot swallow a click meant for the block, a port or a cable. That is already the idiom here — .Port itself is pointer-events: none until a cable is in flight — so the tether is a paint pass, not an affordance. It also means the tether never needs a hit area, which is what would otherwise have made three of them genuinely crowded.

Port view only. The expanded body is full of real blocks and real cables; a faint dashed line threading through it would compete with them and read as wiring. Hidden there, the tether costs nothing.

Routing around, instead of through

You are right that three tethers inside the card is an overload — the interior is the one place a Block has no spare room. Taken outside it costs the interior nothing: out of the row, down the left gutter, along the top border to the port. It is longer, and it spends the left margin, which is otherwise free.

The two that actually work

Rendered, the first two both have a flaw the sketch hid. The direct interior route runs its horizontal along the row's own baseline, so it strikes through the name and the type. And hugging the border outside makes the tether so quiet you cannot follow it. Both are fixed by moving the horizontal off the thing it collides with.

Where I land: interior, in the row gap. The elbow is the clearer wire, as you said — but the reason to keep it inside is that the space outside is not free. Every effect cable already leaves the top edge and travels right, so the margin above the card is that lane. A tether routed around it would run in the same band as the cables it exists to explain, and at three arguments the nested brackets above the card become their own clutter. Inside, dropped into the gap under each row, it touches no text, crosses nothing, and spends a region that is otherwise empty in Port view. Your overload point still stands at the top end: at three tethers even this is busy, and the tether is at its best when one or two arguments mutate — beyond that the port label or the type colour is doing the work and the tether is along for the ride.

The shared-trunk variant, for contrast

What this does to V5's stated costs. The gallery charged it two things. "Interior wiring is a new convention nothing else in the idiom uses" — an elbow is not new; it is the routing every cable already uses, and the dashed weight separates it from a real cable the same way the non-live 18% fade does. "Three tethers converging reads as a tangle" — measured above, they do not converge and do not cross; the curve was doing that. What remains chargeable is subtler: a dashed line inside a card still reads faintly as structure, and on a Block with many rows the verticals get long.
